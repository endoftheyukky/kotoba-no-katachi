# Concept

This project is an experimental web artwork inspired by Seiichi Niikuni
and concrete poetry.

It is NOT a typography generator, motion graphics demo, data visualization,
AI poetry generator, or decorative interactive website.

The central material of the work is language itself.

## Core understanding of concrete poetry

In ordinary writing, language is often treated primarily as a transparent
container for semantic meaning.

Concrete poetry refuses to privilege semantic meaning in this way.

A word simultaneously possesses:

- semantic meaning
- sound / phonetic qualities
- visual form
- spatial position
- scale
- repetition
- rhythm
- density
- absence / whitespace
- relationships to other words
- the order and duration in which it is perceived

Semantic meaning is NOT removed.

Instead, meaning is treated as one property of language among several others.

The work should therefore avoid simply translating meaning into an illustration.

For example:

"遠い" moving far away on screen may be a starting point,
but if the visual result merely illustrates the dictionary meaning of "遠い",
the work remains too literal.

A stronger work allows meaning, sound, letterform, spacing, rhythm and structure
to affect one another.

## Relation to visual art and music

Concrete poetry may approach painting through visual form,
or approach music through sound and rhythm.

However, the intention is not:

poetry + painting
or
poetry + music

The central material remains language.

The work should approach visual art and experimental music
through the manipulation of language itself.

Sound must therefore not behave like background music or conventional sound design.

Visuals must not behave like illustrations placed behind words.

Both sound and image should emerge from the same linguistic material.

## Relation to Niikuni

The visual language should strongly respect the radical simplicity found in
Niikuni's work:

- white background
- predominantly black typography
- language as the primary visible material
- strong use of whitespace
- repetition
- cropping
- fragmentation
- scale
- rotation
- partial glyphs
- density
- disappearance
- isolated character components

Do NOT imitate individual historical works.

The goal is to preserve the underlying attitude while extending what was
historically possible on a fixed printed page.

The computer introduces:

- time
- motion
- algorithmic transformation
- sound
- interaction
- generative rules
- computational relationships between words

These should be considered extensions of concrete-poetic structure,
not added digital effects.

## Important artistic constraint

Every visible or audible transformation should ideally be explainable
through some property of the language itself.

Avoid effects whose only justification is:
"this looks cool."

Examples of possible relationships:

phoneme -> timing
mora -> rhythm
voicing -> sound texture
character structure -> fragmentation
semantic distance -> spatial distance
repetition -> visual / sonic density
word relationships -> composition
glyph structure -> masking / cropping
linguistic transformation -> temporal transformation

These relationships do not need to be scientifically literal.
They may be poetic rules.

But they should be rules.

## Visual direction

Default canvas:

- pure or near-white background
- black typography
- no gradients
- no particles
- no 3D
- no ornamental UI
- no generic glitch effects
- no decorative background
- no illustration

The result should be capable of existing as a convincing static concrete poem
if time were stopped at an arbitrary moment.

Motion should expand the poem rather than compensate for weak composition.

## Sound direction

Think experimental sound poetry rather than soundtrack.

Possible source material:

- pronunciation
- phonemes
- morae
- repetition
- silence
- spacing
- duration
- decomposition of speech into sound

Silence is an active compositional material.

## Long-term research direction

A later version may connect NLP vector representations of language
with concrete-poetic composition.

Word embeddings introduce another property of language:

semantic position in a multidimensional computational space.

The goal is NOT to display an embedding scatter plot.

Instead, invisible semantic relationships may become generative rules
for visible and audible poetic structures.

In short:

invisible semantic space
→ computational rule
→ visible / audible poem

The algorithm must remain subordinate to the poem.
